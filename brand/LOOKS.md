# Looks: directions for a channel's style

A look holds together when it is the rules of **one medium**, written down with numbers, and
guarded by approved reference frames. That is how `brand/frame.md` works: `/setup` writes the
values, `./pal brand test` shows them moving, `./pal brand check --approve` stores the frames
that guard them.

When a creator has no brand yet, offer three of these directions in words, let them pick or mix,
then write `frame.md` from the pick. Each line is a starting point, not a template: the colours
come from the channel's subject, never from this page.

| Direction | Ground | Ink and accent | Type | How things move | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clean studio** (the default) | cool white or graphite planes, flat | one dark ink, one warm accent | a sturdy sans, heavy for titles | land fast, hold still, leave quickly | most talking-head channels |
| **Whiteboard** | off-white with a faint grain | marker black, two marker colours | a hand-drawn or rounded face | lines draw on in the order they are said | lessons, explainers |
| **Blueprint** | deep blue, thin white grid | white lines, one signal colour | a narrow technical sans, small caps for labels | parts assemble; dimension lines extend | engineering, how-it-works |
| **Print shop** | paper colour with visible fibre | two inks that overprint to a third; halftone instead of gradients | a bold grotesque | plates stamp in, slightly off register | culture, history, essays |
| **Terminal** | near-black, no glow | one phosphor colour, one warning colour | monospace only | text types on; blocks wipe by rows | programming, security |
| **Newsroom** | dark neutral with a bright rule | white, one alert colour | a condensed sans for bars, a serif for quotes | bars slide in; numbers tick | news, finance, commentary |
| **Paper cut** | layered coloured paper with soft shadows | the paper colours themselves | a friendly rounded face | layers slide and lift like card | kids, storytelling, culture |
| **Atlas** | sea and land colours, coast outlines | one route colour, one place-name colour | an italic serif for regions, a sans for markers | the camera travels; routes draw | geography, history, travel |

## Rules that apply to every look

- Decide it once, in `frame.md`. An episode never invents a colour or a font.
- One accent, rationed: the one thing to look at.
- Text is read on a phone: nothing meant to be read under 2.6% of the frame's short side.
- Things land and then hold still. Nothing drifts while it is being read.
- Persian and English use the same look: the Persian font is chosen to match the Latin one in
  weight and feel, and layouts mirror for right-to-left.
- Avoid the defaults that make a video look machine-made: a dark void with small things floating
  in it, glow and particles, purple gradients, glass cards, everything fading in at once
  (`.claude/skills/video/hyperframes/spec.md`, "BANNED by default").

## Borrowing a look from somewhere

A reference gives its grammar, never its content: shot lengths, how titles enter, palette roles,
type scale. Describe what you measured, write it as medium rules above, and build it fresh.
Style libraries published for agents can be good to study, but check their licence before
copying any text from them: some are for non-commercial use only, which a monetised channel
cannot use.
